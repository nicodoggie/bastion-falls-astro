## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches how Early Hick talks about what someone wants or is willing
to do:

- `-nat`, the ending for "want to" or "intend to";
- `nater`, the full word for desire, willingness, or intended course;
- `naterlas`, the request word from the last lesson;
- `-'arm`, the ending for being willing or ready to act.

## Wanting with `-nat`

To say someone wants or intends to do an action, Early Hick can replace the
action ending `-'er` with `-nat`.

## The Full Word `nater`

The word `nater` means desire, willingness, or an intended course of action.
Think of it as "wanting" as a full word, not just a word ending.

```txt
nater
want
"desire, intention, intended course"
```

The ending `-nat` comes from this fuller word. That is why the meanings are
close:

```txt
nater
wanting / intended course

-nat
want-to / intend-to ending
```

In the last lesson, you saw `naterlas` in polite requests.

`naterlas` is related to `nater`. It is like saying the request moves "toward
willingness." You do not need to build that word yourself yet. For now, learn it
as the request word.

## Willing with `-'arm`

Early Hick also has `-'arm`, an ending for willingness or readiness. Like
`-nat`, it replaces the regular action ending `-'er`.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"to guide"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-arm"} gloss={"WILLING"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"willing to guide"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

In a sentence:

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-arm"} gloss={"WILLING"} type="suffix" />

This form can sound more formal, pledge-like, or duty-focused than ordinary
wanting with `-nat`.

Use `-nat` when the main idea is desire or intention.

## Try It

Choose the best form.

1. Change `imeresp'er`, "to sleep," into "want to sleep."
1. Change `ward'er`, "to guide," into "willing to guide."
1. Which word makes a request softer: `nater`, `naterlas`, or `wardarm`?
1. Translate: `'al'is-es imerespnat`

<PopQuiz title="Answers">

1. `imerespnat`

   ```txt
   sleep-WANT
   "want to sleep"
   ```

1. `wardarm`

   ```txt
   guide-WILLING
   "willing to guide"
   ```

1. `naterlas`

   ```txt
   REQUEST
   "please / would you be willing to"
   ```

1. `'al'is-es imerespnat`

   ```txt
   person-this-MAIN sleep-WANT
   "I want to sleep."
   ```

</PopQuiz>
