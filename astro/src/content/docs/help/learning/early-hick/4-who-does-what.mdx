## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches the two most important sentence endings:

- `-el`, the doer ending (ergative);
- `-es`, the main noun or receiver ending (absolutive);
- how Early Hick marks sentence jobs with endings instead of English word order.

## The Doer Ending `-el`

When one person or thing does an action to another person or thing, the doer gets `-el`.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"veni"} gloss={"burden"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-tar"} gloss={"UP"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"The handler carries the person."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

`materok` means "handler" or "boatman." The ending `-el` tells you the handler is the one doing the
action.

## The Main Noun Ending `-es`

The ending `-es` marks the main noun of a simple sentence. In a sentence with a doer and a receiver,
it marks the receiver.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
</InterlinearGloss>

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"The person walks."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

In English class terms, this is unusual. English usually treats "the person" in "the person walks"
like a subject. Early Hick marks it with the same ending used for a receiver. The technical term is
absolutive.

## How to Read the Endings

| Ending | Plain name       | Formal name | Job in the sentence             |
| ------ | ---------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------- |
| `-el`  | doer ending      | ergative    | the one doing an action to something |
| `-es`  | main/receiver ending | absolutive | the simple main noun or the receiver |

## Try It

In the sentence below, who is the doer and who is the receiver?

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
</InterlinearGloss>

<PopQuiz title="Answer">

The doer is `materok-el`, "the handler." The receiver is `rismater-es`, "the
reed boat."

A natural English translation is:

```txt
"The handler guides the reed boat."
```

</PopQuiz>
