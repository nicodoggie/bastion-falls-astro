## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches how Early Hick builds larger noun groups:

- `-ul`, the ending for "belonging to";
- where describing words go in a noun group;
- how the main noun still gets the sentence role ending.

## The Belonging Ending `-ul`

To say "the handler's boat," Early Hick puts the owner first and adds `-ul` to
that owner.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossTranslation>{"the handler's reed boat"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The `-ul` ending attaches to the owner, not to the thing owned.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"the handler's"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

<InterlinearGloss
  source={"rismater"}
  gloss={"reed.boat"}
  translation={"reed boat"}
/>

Put them together as `materok-ul rismater`.

## The Owned Noun Still Does Its Job

When the owned noun is part of a sentence, the role ending goes on the owned
noun.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"the handler's reed boat"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

In a full sentence:

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"The handler guides the handler's reed boat."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The owner has `-ul`. The boat has `-es` because it is the receiver of the
action.

## Describing an Owned Noun

Describing words usually come before the noun they describe.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ka-"} gloss={"ADJ"} type="prefix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'il"} gloss={"good"} />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"the handler's good reed boat"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The usual order is:

```txt
owner-ul describing-word noun-role
```

So a longer noun group can still be read one piece at a time:

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ka-"} gloss={"ADJ"} type="prefix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'il"} gloss={"good"} />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"this good reed boat of the handler"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

## Belonging Is Not Always Ownership

The same `-ul` ending can mark ordinary ownership, close association, or family
relationship. English often uses "of" or "'s" for the same range.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"tan"} gloss={"self"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"vinud"} gloss={"dwelling"} />
  <GlossTranslation>{"one's own dwelling"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-ul"} gloss={"BELONG"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"vinud"} gloss={"dwelling"} />
  <GlossTranslation>{"the person's dwelling"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

Use the fuller noun or personal-reference form when you need to make the owner
clear. In ordinary speech, nearby objects can also be understood from context
without an explicit "my" or "your."

## Try It

Translate each noun group.

1. `materok-ul rismater`
1. `materok-ul rismater-es`
1. `materok-ul ka-'il rismater-es`
1. `tan-ul vinud`

<PopQuiz title="Answers">

1. `materok-ul rismater`

   ```txt
   handler-BELONG reed.boat
   "the handler's reed boat"
   ```

1. `materok-ul rismater-es`

   ```txt
   handler-BELONG reed.boat-RECEIVER
   "the handler's reed boat" as a receiver/main noun
   ```

1. `materok-ul ka-'il rismater-es`

   ```txt
   handler-BELONG ADJ-good reed.boat-RECEIVER
   "the handler's good reed boat" as a receiver/main noun
   ```

1. `tan-ul vinud`

   ```txt
   self-BELONG dwelling
   "one's own dwelling"
   ```

</PopQuiz>
