## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches how Early Hick joins equal pieces:

- `o ... o ru`, for "and";
- `o ... o 'a`, for "or";
- `o ... o ewes`, for "together with";
- how the joined group gets one sentence role ending.

## Mark Each Listed Item with `o`

Early Hick uses `o` to mark items in a list. The joining word comes after the
listed items.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"ru"} gloss={"and"} />
  <GlossTranslation>{"this person and the handler"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The word `o` does not mean "and" by itself. It marks each listed item. The
joining word `ru` tells you the list means "and."

That final joining word speaks for the whole list. It is also where Early Hick
puts the role ending for the joined group.

## And with `ru`

When the joined group does the action, put the doer ending `-el` on `ru`.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"ru"} gloss={"and"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"This person and the handler guide the reed boat."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The people in the list do not each need `-el`. The ending goes on `ru` because
`ru` carries the role of the whole joined group.

When the joined group is the main noun or receiver, use `ru-es`.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"ru"} gloss={"and"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"The handler guides this person and the reed boat."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

## Or with `'a`

Use `'a` when the joined items are alternatives, like English "or."

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"materok"} gloss={"handler"} />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"'a"} gloss={"or"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"rismater"} gloss={"reed.boat"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"guide"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"This person or the handler guides the reed boat."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The same role-ending pattern applies:

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'a"} gloss={"or"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-el"} gloss={"DOER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"one of them as the doer"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'a"} gloss={"or"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"RECEIVER"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"one of them as the receiver or main noun"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The older sense behind `'a` is "other" or "alternative." In ordinary sentences
like these, read it as "or."

## Together with `ewes`

Use `ewes` when the point is shared action or equal participation. It is closer
to "together with" than plain "and."

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"Mari"} gloss={"Mari"} />
  <GlossToken source={"o"} gloss={"LIST"} />
  <GlossToken source={"ewes"} gloss={"together"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ward"} gloss={"travel"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"This person and Mari travel together."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

Here `ewes-es` treats the listed people as one shared-action group.

The word `ewes` can also mean "together with" outside lists. In this pattern,
it is the final joining word for the whole group.

## Try It

Choose the best joining word or translate the phrase.

1. Which word means ordinary "and": `ru`, `'a`, or `ewes`?
1. Which word means "or": `ru`, `'a`, or `ewes`?
1. Translate: `'al'is o materok o ru-el`
1. Translate: `'al'is o Mari o ewes-es`

<PopQuiz title="Answers">

1. `ru`

   ```txt
   and
   ```

1. `'a`

   ```txt
   or
   ```

1. `'al'is o materok o ru-el`

   ```txt
   person-this LIST handler LIST and-DOER
   "this person and the handler" as doers
   ```

1. `'al'is o Mari o ewes-es`

   ```txt
   person-this LIST Mari LIST together-MAIN
   "this person together with Mari" as one shared group
   ```

</PopQuiz>
