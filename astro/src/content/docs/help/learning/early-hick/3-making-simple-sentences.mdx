## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches three beginner patterns:

- many Early Hick words begin as noun-like ideas;
- the ending `-'er` turns a word into an action word;
- a simple sentence can say that one person or thing does an action.

## Nouns and Action Words

In English, "walk" is already an action word. Early Hick often marks action words with the ending
`-'er`.

```txt
barak
walk
```

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"\"walks\" or \"is walking\""}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The technical name for `-'er` is a verbalizer. In this book, call it the action ending.

## A First Sentence

Early Hick uses the ending `-es` on the main person or thing in a simple sentence. You will learn
more about this ending in the next lesson.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"The person walks."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

For now, read `-es` as "this is the main noun of the sentence."

## Leaving Out Known People

Early Hick often leaves out a personal word when everyone already knows who is being talked about. A
bare action can work like English "I walk" or "they walk" when the story has already made the person
clear.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"\"I walk\" or \"they walk,\" if context makes the person clear"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The fuller form uses `'al`, "person," plus a "this" ending. It is stronger, like pointing to "this
person" in the sentence.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"'al'is"} gloss={"this.person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"\"I walk\" with extra emphasis"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

Writers and poets use the fuller forms more often for rhythm, contrast, or a stronger voice.

## More Action Words

| Base word | Basic meaning | With `-'er` | Sentence meaning |
| --------- | ------------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| `barak`   | walk          | `barak'er`  | walks            |
| `ward`    | guide         | `ward'er`   | guides           |
| `his`     | see           | `his'er`    | sees             |
| `'irek`   | stand, firm   | `'irek'er`  | stands           |

## Try It

Add the action ending `-'er`, then translate the sentence.

```txt
'ales ward'er
```

<PopQuiz title="Answer">

`ward` means "guide." `ward'er` means "guides."
