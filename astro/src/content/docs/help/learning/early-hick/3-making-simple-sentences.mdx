## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches three beginner patterns:

- many Early Hick words begin as noun-like ideas;
- the ending `-'er` turns a word into an action word;
- a simple sentence can say that one person or thing does an action.

## Nouns and Action Words

In English, "walk" is already an action word. Early Hick often marks action
words with the ending `-'er`.

```txt
barak
walk
```

```txt
barak'er
walk-ACTION
"walks" or "is walking"
```

The technical name for `-'er` is a verbalizer. In this book, call it the action
ending.

## A First Sentence

Early Hick uses the ending `-es` on the main person or thing in a simple
sentence. You will learn more about this ending in the next lesson.

```txt
'ales barak'er
person-MAIN walk-ACTION
"The person walks."
```

For now, read `-es` as "this is the main noun of the sentence."

## Leaving Out Known People

Early Hick often leaves out a personal word when everyone already knows who is
being talked about. A bare action can work like English "I walk" or "they walk"
when the story has already made the person clear.

```txt
barak'er
walk-ACTION
"I walk" or "they walk," if context makes the person clear
```

The fuller form uses `'al`, "person," plus a "this" ending. It is stronger,
like pointing to "this person" in the sentence.

```txt
'al'ises barak'er
this.person-MAIN walk-ACTION
"I walk" with extra emphasis
```

Writers and poets use the fuller forms more often for rhythm, contrast, or a
stronger voice.

## Known They and General They

Early Hick can also show whether "they" means a known group or a more general,
distant group.

```txt
ma'altalel ward'er
known.people-DOER guide-ACTION
"They guide" (those people we know or have been talking about)
```

```txt
ma'alrumel ward'er
distant.people-DOER guide-ACTION
"They guide" (people like that, not a specific nearby group)
```

## More Action Words

| Base word | Basic meaning | With `-'er` | Sentence meaning |
| --------- | ------------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| `barak`   | walk          | `barak'er`  | walks            |
| `ward`    | guide         | `ward'er`   | guides           |
| `his`     | see           | `his'er`    | sees             |
| `'irek`   | stand, firm   | `'irek'er`  | stands           |

## Try It

Add the action ending `-'er`, then translate the sentence.

```txt
'ales ward'er
```

<PopQuiz title="Answer">

`ward` means "guide." `ward'er` means "guides."

```txt
'ales ward'er
person-MAIN guide-ACTION
"The person guides."
```

</PopQuiz>
