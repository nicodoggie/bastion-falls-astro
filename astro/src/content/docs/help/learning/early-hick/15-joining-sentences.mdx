## What You'll Learn

This lesson teaches how Early Hick links one idea to another:

- `-'er-es`, an action turned into an event noun;
- `'aterimris`, "after";
- `'aterbran`, "before";
- `'atertel`, "until";
- `imris`, "because";
- `las`, "despite" or "even though."

## From Action to Event

When Early Hick uses one action as the time frame for another, the action often
gets the main noun ending `-es`.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"walks"}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"\"walking\" / \"the walking event\""}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

This lets the action work like a noun phrase inside a larger sentence. In the
technical grammar, this is a nominalized verb: an action word used as a noun.

## After with `'aterimris`

Use `'aterimris` after the event that happens first.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'aterimris,"} gloss={"after"} />
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"imeresp"} gloss={"sleep"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"After walking, this person sleeps."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The first part gives the time frame. The second part gives the main sentence.

## Before with `'aterbran`

Use `'aterbran` after the event that has not happened yet.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'aterbran,"} gloss={"before"} />
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"imeresp"} gloss={"sleep"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"Before walking, this person sleeps."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The structure is the same as the "after" pattern:

```txt
event-es time-link, main sentence
```

## Until with `'atertel`

Use `'atertel` when one event continues up to another event. English often puts
"until" before the second event. Early Hick can put the time link after that
event instead.

## Because with `imris`

Use `imris` when one sentence gives the reason for another.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"imris"} gloss={"because"} />
  <GlossToken source={"toran"} gloss={"river"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ter"} gloss={"flow"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er,"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"Because the river flows, this person walks."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

Here, `imris` introduces the reason. The sentence after the comma gives what
happens because of that reason.

## Despite with `las`

Use `las` when the first sentence gives something that does not stop the main
sentence from happening. English often says "despite" with a noun, or "even
though" with a full sentence.

<InterlinearGloss>
  <GlossToken source={"las"} gloss={"despite"} />
  <GlossToken source={"toran"} gloss={"river"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"ter"} gloss={"flow"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er,"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"'al"} gloss={"person"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'is"} gloss={"this"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"-es"} gloss={"MAIN"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossToken source={"barak"} gloss={"walk"} />
  <GlossToken source={"-'er"} gloss={"ACTION"} type="suffix" />
  <GlossTranslation>{"Even though the river flows, this person walks."}</GlossTranslation>
</InterlinearGloss>

The same word is related to the place ending `-las`, "into." In this sentence,
it works as a sentence link, not as a place ending.

## The Link Can Move

In beginner examples, put the time or reason link first when it helps the reader
follow the sentence:

```txt
barak'er-es 'aterimris, 'al'is-es imeresp'er
after walking, this person sleeps
```

```txt
imris toran-es ter'er, 'al'is-es barak'er
because the river flows, this person walks
```

Early Hick can move these pieces for style or story flow. A link at the
beginning puts the relationship in focus. A link later in the sentence can feel
more like ordinary story flow.

## Try It

Choose the best link.

1. Say "after walking."
1. Say "before walking."
1. Say "until sleeping."
1. Which word means "because": `'aterbran`, `'aterimris`, or `imris`?
1. Which word means "despite" or "even though": `las` or `ru`?
1. Translate: `imris toran-es ter'er`

<PopQuiz title="Answers">

1. `barak'er-es 'aterimris`

   ```txt
   walk-ACTION-MAIN after
   "after walking"
   ```

1. `barak'er-es 'aterbran`

   ```txt
   walk-ACTION-MAIN before
   "before walking"
   ```

1. `imeresp'er-es 'atertel`

   ```txt
   sleep-ACTION-MAIN until
   "until sleeping"
   ```

1. `imris`

   ```txt
   because
   ```

1. `las`

   ```txt
   despite / even though
   ```

1. `imris toran-es ter'er`

   ```txt
   because river-MAIN flow-ACTION
   "because the river flows"
   ```

</PopQuiz>
