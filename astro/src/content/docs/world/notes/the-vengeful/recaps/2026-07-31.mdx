## Previously, in *The Vengeful*…

The voyage to rescue House Rannek became a pursuit—not merely across Maiden’s Bay, but into the
Rannek Marches, where ships, soldiers, magistrates, and even familiar faces could no longer be
trusted.

Aboard the *Illegally Bear*, the party continued toward Port Mibram under Marchioness Maria Rannek’s
authority. Somewhere ahead, Marquess Uther Rannek, Lance, and Christian Marlion remained beyond
contact. Behind them lay the compromised departure from Port Surrey, the invisible observer who had
learned their faces, and the Reformist submarine carrying black powder toward an unknown
destination.

The sea initially offered little proof of pursuit. A distant vessel remained astern without drawing
closer, and Sapphire kept watch from the bow while the pirate crew settled into its usual rhythm.
Crewman Yannick offered her a suspicious pouch of crushed mushrooms and herbs—supposedly a tea-based
party substance capable of sustaining a night’s revelry. Sapphire sensibly declined to consume
unidentified pirate powder while on duty and kept it for later investigation.

Between dangers, the voyage made room for stranger and more personal matters. Sapphire spoke of Fey
mating bonds: sacred and uncommon connections, far removed from ordinary courtship. She described
the Hags of Hama as dangerous but generally faithful to the literal terms of their bargains—a
reassurance undermined by the fact that literal hag bargains remain exceptionally good at destroying
people through details they failed to ask about.

She also spoke of her home: Hell’s Kitchen, a brothel near a peaceful lake in Maple Bridge, not far
from Pumpkin Town. Her mother raised Sapphire and her sisters to protect themselves, endure
hardship, and remain kind where kindness remained possible. In the Autumn Court, Pumpkin Town comes
alive with the season beneath the legend of a Pumpkin King said to consume nightmares.

The peace above Maiden’s Bay did not last.

The sky darkened directly over the party as a magical discharge began to form. A *counterspell*
stopped the first lightning bolt, leaving motes of St. Elmo’s fire burning overhead. Sapphire traced
the attack to an elderly spellcaster standing aboard a submarine in the open bay.

Andrew recognized him as one of the Malthrek court wizards—a man who had once delivered documents
during Andrew’s training. Crispin Mallow had physically switched the wizard’s legs, leaving them
stitched into reversed positions. The result resembled the backward-foot signs previously associated
with Oren Bell, though the resemblance did not yet explain Mallow’s purpose.

Sapphire manifested a green-and-gold longbow and struck the wizard through the neck. Unseen hands
dragged him back through the hatch before the submarine submerged. Andrew’s next shot found only the
hull.

Oren had already placed a tracking spell upon the fleeing vessel or someone aboard it, but the
submarine dove for deeper water and dispelled the effect. Sapphire continued the chase through a
summoned shark, seeing through its senses as it followed the submarine along the dredged channels of
the bay. The vessel turned its magical water propulsion against the shark, but the animal escaped
and hid among others near the reef before resuming pursuit from a safer distance.

Sapphire recruited another watcher as well: a seagull, paid with an enormous tuna caught from her
Feywild fishing rod. The bird reported that the submarine’s occupants had already attacked other
gulls using lightning magic that stimulated the clouds and called down bolts. Several had been, in
the seagull’s technical assessment, “cooked”—meaning spectacularly scorched rather than transformed
into dinner.

Captain Illegal E. Bear confirmed that similar submarines had been striking ships with lightning,
damaging or slowing their masts while still allowing them to limp into port. Their purpose remained
unclear: piracy, disruption of commerce, pursuit of the Ranneks, or some wider operation against
Senera.

Until then, Bear had believed that his passengers were ordinary contraband. Hellion finally told him
the truth: the supposed merchandise were nobles rescued from the Reformist Church. He warned Bear
that the Reformists targeted yellow-eyed people, including children, and harvested the livers of
those sensitive to magic for their rituals.

Bear wanted no part of the Church.

The party agreed to defend the *Illegally Bear* in return for cooperation and an additional payment
after docking. They insisted upon entering Port Mibram through the official docks rather than
disappearing into another pirate cove.

Bear then revealed the ship’s concealed identity mechanism: an enormous clockwork contraption,
attributed to Racos Palma, capable of replacing its figurehead and banners. The notorious teddy bear
vanished, and the vessel became the *Nightshade*.

By late afternoon, the disguised vessel reached Port Mibram. Tug crews and dockworkers reacted
nervously, unknown freight began moving across the docks, and someone called for the constabulary.
The ship’s figurehead had become a generic mermaid, whether through another disguise or some
unexplained mechanical choice.

Oren identified a dockside watcher bearing the same reversed-foot signature that had followed the
party before. Concluding that the port was already searching for them, he unleashed fey chaos and
illusory fugitives to draw pursuit away. The rest of the party escaped inland by carriage while Oren
remained behind near the armory, dealing with threats and warning them away from flesh golems.

Hellion bought the *Illegally Bear* roughly one hour in port using a signed copy of *Legally Bare*.
The magazine reportedly bore the signatures of three models. Lollia Skala had appeared in its first
issue as a guest for cross-promotion rather than as one of its regular models.

Hellion then proposed a new business venture to Captain Bear.

The recently conceived *Ramboys* magazine could serve as cover for a black-market Herb of the Harem
trade, bypassing the Maidenfeld monopoly in both Senera and Apgar. Rather than importing the herb
from Ahuria, druids might reproduce the orb-rich conditions in which it grows. Natural orb deposits
are believed to exist in Senera, and synthesized orbs are known elsewhere, though none have been
documented locally.

The magazine itself had already received Management approval, though it was not yet ready to
publish. Using it as a coded contraband network was another matter and would require risk-management
review.

Bear, meanwhile, already possessed Herb of the Harem. He swallowed one of its comparatively mild,
processed pills and offered the far stronger flowers for sale. Whether Bear’s flowers were genuine
remained uncertain.

From Port Mibram, the party travelled toward Rannekford disguised as petitioners. Their driver
reported that Hicklander rebels in Maiden’s Field were destroying weirs and flood-control works,
isolating the Marquess’s forces. He repeated rumours that Lance Rannek had been captured and
speculated that the rebels had Apgarian support, but neither claim was verified.

Castle Rannek’s approaches were unusually fortified. Guards crowded bridges and entrances, and Maria
considered the deployments abnormal. Hellion and Sapphire detected an unidentified magical influence
affecting at least some of them.

At the checkpoint, the party invented a story about murdered merchants and stolen spices to secure
urgent entry. Brief truesight showed the guards themselves to be physically natural, but one carried
a strange pink enchantment haze. After a monk used *Hand of Healing* upon him, the guard recognized
Gwyniviere as “Your Grace”—then obeyed her instruction to preserve her disguise.

Registered under false names, the party entered the magistrates’ offices.

There, truesight exposed Magistrate Tonon as a Face-Stealer.

With Gwyniviere’s assistance, the group subdued the creature and confirmed its nature. The false
Tonon carried a false tooth filled with poison, intended to be bitten so the agent could die rather
than divulge information. It also bore an unexplained metallic feature.

Its room contained a ward and an Orb of Enhancement. The party removed the orb, relocated the
captive and its belongings, and entered Tonon’s stolen memories.

The real Magistrate Tonon was dead.

An unidentified cloaked figure had murdered him and somehow furnished the Face-Stealer with his
memories. To exploit the opening, Trajan agreed to impersonate the impostor Tonon and help obtain
urgent access to Marchioness Maria Rannek—or, more precisely, to whichever Maria currently occupied
Castle Rannek.

By then, the crisis had become a war of duplicates.

Oren reported puppet constructs throughout Rannekford. A flesh golem resembling Mark Malthrek and
another figure resembling Gwyniviere were travelling toward the naval or admiralty offices,
apparently attempting to send forces toward Maiden’s Field. False copies of Maria Rannek, Mark, and
Gwyniviere were all believed to be active, though the party could not yet say whether each was a
Face-Stealer, one of Mallow’s lifelike constructs, or something else.

Magistrate Wessen appeared sincere and agreed to seek an audience with Maria. She also reported that
a Malthrek and a Malthrek court wizard had teleported a Maria into the castle.

The account was secondhand. Bantam had personally seen only the Maria.

The reported Malthrek was identified as Luke, while Stacy Cartwright was suggested as the
accompanying wizard. But the real Maria was travelling with the party, and the real Luke was at the
Travertines. The Maria inside Castle Rannek was therefore treated as an impostor, while the supposed
Luke might be another of Mallow’s flesh golems.

Stacy’s part was less clear. Her apparent return to Castle Malthrek had been unexpected, raising the
possibility that Mallow had already compromised her. Whether she acted freely, under coercion, or
after being altered or replaced remained unknown.

Amid these revelations, Gwyniviere confirmed that her engagement to Mark Malthrek was over. Mark had
first written—on Emily Hazeldine’s advice—to arrange a face-to-face conversation rather than
callously ending the engagement by letter. Hellion had initially misunderstood the message and
jokingly blamed Mark’s “evil mistress,” meaning Emily.

The party established the Spring Dancer Inn as a staging point while Oren continued scouting.

There, Sandor attempted a *sending* to Angel:

The castle was being taken. He had to go—

The spell ended before he could finish, and before Angel could answer.

Oren soon reported that stitched-leg puppet constructs could recognize him despite his disguises. He
renewed *nondetection* and urged the bishop to secure the cathedral with *hallow*.

Sapphire, meanwhile, left a moving carriage to confront two watchers. She destroyed the first
watcher’s spyglass and struck them repeatedly without holding back. They were left at least
unconscious and likely dead, though their nature was never established.

The second tried to cast a spell. Sapphire interrupted it, closed with her magical scimitar, and
defeated them. Their shifting face confirmed that this watcher was a Face-Stealer.

At Rannek Cathedral, the party sought Bishop Magnus Bramok. The complex felt continually blessed,
although no active *hallow* could be confirmed. Angel encountered a man whose health had remained
greatly improved since she removed a cursed token from him and healed him with celestial power.

The bishop and his allies were warned about Face-Stealers, parasites, and the possible fall of
Castle Rannek. Kelthyr Kulok insisted that the Hicklanders as a whole were not allied with the
Reformist Church. Their movement consists of decentralized, compartmentalized groups; some cells may
be infiltrated or independently allied with the Reformists without implicating every other cell.

Kelthyr maintains liaisons across several such groups, attempting to synchronize compatible efforts
without exposing or merging them. The bishop proposed warning clergy as far as Maiden’s Field, while
the party considered using the cathedral bells as an emergency alarm and sheltering civilians within
the cathedral complex.

The immediate objective is now clear: secure Castle Rannek before its impostors can command the
household, compromise its servants, or redirect Seneran forces toward Maiden’s Field.

Gwyniviere knows secret entrances used during painting sessions with Angel, but wants their
locations closely held. Wessen agreed to search for Sandor. Maria’s signet ring can establish her
physical authority even if magical messages and naval authentication codes have been compromised.

Angel also revealed that her power has grown. Kelthyr taught her *dimension door*, and after the
events at the Travertines she felt a surge allowing her to cast more powerful magic. Hellion carries
two teleportation scrolls, reserved for dire emergencies.

The party has divided again.

Hellion, Sir Hilis Brastok, Angel, Gwyniviere, Branrum Malterdale, and Erik Malmann will form the
castle team.

Oren Bell, Marchioness Maria Rannek, Sapphire, Andrew Taminok, and Sister Pira Tradewise will form
the naval team.

The real Tonon is dead. A Face-Stealer wore his life and memories.

A false Maria may already sit inside Castle Rannek. Copies of Mark and Gwyniviere are attempting to
move naval forces. Sandor’s warning was cut short. Lance may be a prisoner. Stacy may be
compromised. Mallow’s constructs can recognize Oren through disguise, while the submarine operation
continues somewhere along Senera’s coast.

The party has reached the Ranneks.

Now they must retake a castle whose enemies have already learned to wear its faces.
