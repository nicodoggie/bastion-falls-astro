## Previously, in *The Vengeful*…

The road to Maiden’s Field has become a road through Crispin Mallow’s handiwork.

Hellion Blanchimontt, Sir Erik Malmann, Oren Bell, and Klaus continued east beneath that gathering
shadow. Their first warning was not an army, but a shattered produce carriage: one horse gone, the
other pinned and bleeding beneath its yoke, its owners nowhere in sight. Hellion and Oren freed and
healed the mare, Lacey. Blood, torn clothing, and a trail through the hills revealed what had become
of the traders.

Klaus led the party into a narrow ravine, where four armed captors guarded two bound prisoners in a
wooden cage. The captors believed moonlight had given the traders a blue cast and marked them as
chosen by the Lord. The traders themselves showed no yellow-eyed sign of magic.

Then Oren’s eyes burned yellow. Purplish-green markings spread across him like luminous tentacles,
and the captors mistook the eldritch display for the arrival of the Reformist acolyte they had been
expecting. They lowered their weapons. They knelt.

Oren used their error to end the confrontation without a battle. He ordered the traders released and
persuaded the four captors to abandon the Reformists. Someone unknown had already unlocked the cage.
The battered traders accepted healing and water, then left for their village—but not before
privately mentioning changelings and refusing to say what they knew.

Their captors proved to be displaced villagers rather than hardened zealots. They said Hicklanders
had burned their settlement after they refused to join the rebellion. A captured Reformist operative
offered a competing story: bandits had committed the attack, and the Reformists had merely exploited
its survivors. Neither account has been proved. What is certain is what followed. The Reformists fed
and sheltered the villagers’ families at Goat’s Head, promised them new homes, and turned gratitude
and fear into obedience.

Dean, the recruits’ apparent leader, feared for his daughter Carissa, who had gone away to “become a
sister.” She had once been reckless. After Reformist instruction, she had become obedient enough to
alarm those who knew her. Hellion warned Dean that obedience might conceal coercion, conditioning,
or worse. Testimony from the recruits and their prisoner described public whippings disguised as
purity rites, revelry used to break down young initiates, and sexual exploitation presented as proof
of devotion. Whether Carissa has been subjected to magic, a parasite, or any other particular method
remains unknown.

Oren showed the recruits a more literal instrument of control: a parasite preserved in a flask. The
operative called such creatures “seeds,” meant to advance humanity’s ascension. The live parasite
inside him reacted violently whenever Oren brought the flask near, forcing its host into spasms. He
also carried paralytic darts, a bone saw, and a butcher’s knife.

The operative finally spoke of a male superior already in Maiden’s Field, “playing the gardener
again.” He never named Crispin Mallow, but the words recalled Mallow’s old flower-contractor
disguise at Castle Malthrek. He begged to be killed before his cooperation could be discovered and
before he could be made into “that patchwork.” His throat was cut beside a dead willow, and his body
was hidden inside it. The surrendered recruits recoiled from the execution.

They nevertheless chose to accompany the party toward Maiden’s Field. Oren ordered them to help save
Carissa. Hellion’s group could not promise an immediate rescue of every family at Goat’s Head, but
it could seek money, mercenaries, and allies. Sir Malmann, asked not to preach, answered with
silence and a prayer for the people still in danger.

Elsewhere, the consequences of Mallow’s work were becoming harder to hide.

At Castle Rannek, Stacy remained alive after the removal of the organs, limbs, and pieces of Auraxx
that had been unnaturally joined to her. She was still recovering beneath Sister Pira’s care.
Another missing guard returned with his legs switched, set himself and a building ablaze, and showed
how Mallow could turn a stolen person into both a weapon and a message. Castle personnel responded
with increasingly invasive identity examinations while families waited for news of forty-seven
people who had not come home.

One of them soon did.

Marchioness Maria Rannek sent Angel to investigate the Lord’s Judgment, a registered mercenary guild
operating from a weapon-covered tavern. Sir Hilis Brastok, Trajan—still publicly
wearing Hellion’s face—and Rannek soldiers accompanied her. The guild’s leader, Bertram Dorand,
verified their authority and allowed them to search.

Magic led the investigators into a locked basement filled with supplies, trophies, gifts, and snow
globes. Hidden among them was a black-purple Orb of Enhancement. Opening the room released magic
resembling the Reformists’ worms.

Angel used *Locate Creature* to pursue whoever had placed the orb. The chase ended on a rooftop,
where *Hold Person* stopped a fleeing man before he could leap to another building. He was Leland, a
genuine missing Rannek soldier. His legs had been switched. Ordinary thread entered his altered body
through a magical needle, allowing Mallow to move him like a puppet. An evocation glyph lay waiting
inside his chest until Angel dispelled it.

A second magical search led across the river to a spellcaster wearing Kethbjorn’s face. He defended
himself with *Shield*, but an augmented command—“Drop”—brought him down. Telepathy proved that he
had neither Kethbjorn’s memories nor Kethbjorn’s love for Angel. His name was Lotan.

Lotan was another captive, not the architect of the crime.

At the sound of Mallow’s name, memories broke through. Lotan remembered being forced into his
Face-Stealer form, made to consume another person’s facial skin, and driven through more changes
than his body could safely endure. He remembered Mallow as a concealed-faced man with bright golden
wings and flesh made partly draconic by his own grafts.

Through those memories, Angel saw the prisoners: Uther Rannek, the real Kethbjorn, Sandor, and
several court wizards, each confined in a separate cage. Uther was badly injured and desperately
weak. Sandor had been severely beaten. Their captor had hurt them, but had not yet overcome their
minds.

Angel used the connection to threaten Mallow in return. She invoked Minfilia, Oscar, Hellion, and
Sapphire and promised to deliver him to Minfilia alive. Then she withdrew before learning whether he
could answer through Lotan.

Lotan’s memories also revealed Face-Stealers tortured and conditioned from childhood: stripped of
identity, trained to become other people, and taught to serve the Divine Masochist. Angel told him
that his body and identity belonged to him. Trajan accepted responsibility for his care, with Sister
Pira and Castle Rannek’s healing pool offering the best hope the party presently possessed.

The discovery changed the mission. Face-Stealers altered and controlled by Mallow would be treated
as captives to disable and rescue, not enemies to slaughter. Leland’s recovery reduced the count of
the missing from forty-seven to forty-six. The others now had a witness who had seen where at least
some of them were held.

Angel returned to the Lord’s Judgment and hired all twenty members for two months. The company
agreed on the condition that it would not participate in persecution. Bertram placed its fighters
under Sir Hilis’s operational command.

A search of Lance Rannek’s room produced a key marked “Rannekford Storage Company” and one of his
early wooden giraffe carvings. *Locate Creature* pointed toward Maiden’s Field—and then abruptly
lost him. Hellion believed Lance was alive, but admitted that this was hope and intuition, not
proof.

Then Duke Ellington and Duchess Antoinette Malthrek arrived at Castle Rannek with Katalin and two
guards. They passed the castle’s Face-Stealer protocol, apologized for the harm they had caused
while compromised, and promised restitution. Repeated uses of *Encode Thoughts* and
*Greater Restoration* were still returning memories stolen by long parasite exposure.

Those memories described an infiltration measured not in days, but years. Tyrion’s superior had been
replaced by an infiltrator roughly eighteen months earlier, perhaps after an earlier copy was
punished and replaced for making a mistake. Magistrates had been targeted. Gwyniviere and Mark
Malthrek had both been substituted. A golem duplicate of Mark remained imprisoned, while the real
Mark had lost about a year of his life to the parasite in his head.

Ellington and Antoinette announced that once restitution had begun, they would abdicate. Mark—not
his elder brother Luke—would become Duke of Malthrek, with Emily Hazeldine expected to marry him and
help lead the court wizards.

In the war room, Katalin named Crispin Mallow as the Reformists’ current Candlebearer. Whether that
public office reflects his true rank remains unresolved. He may be an ascended Dark Archon answering
directly to Sovereign Rothschild, but that remains celestial intelligence and theory rather than
established fact.

What the council could describe more confidently was Mallow’s method. His magical needle could pass
ordinary thread through bodies altered by his fleshcraft, binding them into puppets. The technique
could control Face-Stealers he had unnaturally modified; it was not an inherent weakness of ordinary
Face-Stealers. His experiments consumed dragon bone, scales, Auraxx’s manifested anatomy, and pieces
of the Heart of the Urdefolcin. The golden wings seen in Lotan’s memories were not proof of
divinity. They were another thing Mallow had made from someone else.

The council suspected that Mallow was exploiting the Hicklander rebellion to pursue a vendetta
against the Maidenfelds. It also considered whether Angel herself was a target, perhaps for
*Planar Binding*. Neither motive has been proved. Direct teleportation to Uther was rejected as too
dangerous; a fleshcrafter who turns captives into traps could prepare the destination just as
carefully.

Reports from Kurtis showed the same pattern at a different scale. Pablito Escobar and Torkin
remained in custody. The mayor faced trial for corruption, graft, and coercive misconduct involving
minors. The Band of the Ladle held the evidence, and a protective squad was on its way. Sapphire,
Andrew, Thilas, Airur, Tormil, and James had left the settlement under their Taminok Brewery cover
and continued toward Maiden’s Field.

The Reformists, however, were not one perfectly coordinated machine. Their cells had infiltrated the
Admiralty, Malthrek forces, Eastonton’s Paladinate, and parts of the Hicklander movements over
different periods and often operated independently. Compromised Duke Ellington had tried to unite
those efforts because he understood their fragmentation as a weakness. That did not give him command
of every cell, and it does not mean every Hicklander, soldier, noble, or church member serves the
same design.

At Port Mibram, that infiltration had left Vice Marshal Tyrion Donmar as the Western Admiralty’s
only remaining marshal. The Marshal of the Fleet and the marshal responsible for the Malter River
were missing. Maria promoted Tyrion to Marshal for taking command, opposing his compromised
superior, and protecting what remained.

The port also held something the rescue force desperately needed: an immense *Teleportation Circle*.
Hellion’s Orbs of Enhancement had already been installed in its conduits, but the military casters
did not know how to draw upon them. Angel altered one corner of the sigil into a triangle, channeled
the orbs through herself, and awakened the circle without spending a spell slot. She then taught a
military caster how to direct the spell through the orbs. Left in place, they should recharge.

For the first time, the scattered defenders possessed a route capable of moving reinforcements,
supplies, refugees, and rescued captives between allied circles. Angel ordered Port Mibram guarded
and its sigil shared with allies.

Now the divided roads are beginning to converge.

Hellion’s group approaches Maiden’s Field with four former Reformist recruits, a warning about a
“gardener,” and families at Goat’s Head still waiting to be saved. Sapphire and Andrew approach
under the colors of a brewery. Angel intends to bring Gwyniviere, Hilis, Bertram, and the Lord’s
Judgment to Laudine Maidenfeld’s estate outside the city. Maria has declared their expedition an
official rescue mission.

Uther, Kethbjorn, Sandor, the court wizards, Lance, and dozens of others remain missing. Mallow has
used their bodies, faces, loyalties, and institutions to isolate everyone who might oppose him. But
Leland has been recovered. Lotan has been offered his own name. The Admiralty can move again. The
mercenaries have signed their contract. And the people approaching Maiden’s Field now know that the
figures wearing enemy faces may be prisoners still waiting to come home.

The road leads toward a gardener who cultivates obedience from terror and new flesh from stolen
lives—whether that gardener is Mallow himself or another superior using his old disguise.

This time, the rescue force is coming for the harvest.
