## Previously, in *The Vengeful*…

The struggle for House Rannek has changed again.

At first, the danger seemed to be a siege: retake Castle Rannek, expose the impostors within it, and
restore the Marchioness to her own throne. But once the castle was secured, the party found the
deeper wound. This was not merely an attack upon a fortress. It was a campaign to make every
familiar face, every official order, and every safe road into a possible weapon.

Marchioness Maria Rannek and Vice Marshal Tyrion Donmar established a perimeter outside the
recaptured castle. Most of their regular forces remained committed toward Maiden's Field, leaving
the castle protected, but thinly staffed. Inside, Stacy Cartwright lay unconscious and terribly
mangled. Whatever force had been controlling her had released its hold, but freedom had not yet
meant healing.

Hellion called the available leadership to the situation room. Maria came with Donmar and
Gwyniviere; Mulberry was placed in temporary command of the remaining guards, with Bramble among
those still on duty. Yet the guard captain, Captain Blueberry, Sandor Kethlas, and the entire court
wizardry had vanished.

The party began by testing what could be tested. Trajan examined Mulberry and Bramble for the
distinctive anatomy of Face-Stealers and found none. Their memories, manner, and genuine surprise at
Angel's earlier arrival supported their identities as well. But this was only a beginning. A person
could pass an anatomical inspection and still be a changeling, another shapeshifter, a prisoner
under coercion, or simply someone bought by the enemy. Castle Rannek's defenses had been built to
stop an army—not an enemy who knew its codes, copied its people, and perhaps understood its habits
better than the castle understood itself.

In the guest quarters, the evidence became more personal. A room associated with Stacy held magical
blood across the bed and a small robe that Angel recognized as Sandor's. The blood did not prove a
mortal wound, but Sandor himself remained missing. Hellion recovered an uncursed Bag of Holding from
the room, its owner unknown. Another room held an ordinary spellbook and a chair clumsily repaired
after what looked like a struggle: another small sign that somebody had been taken in haste.

Ulfa's rooms told a similarly fractured story. Her star chart and outward-facing astrolabe remained
in place, but her crystal ball had been replaced with a chipped, broken substitute. Witnesses had
seen “Ulfa” in several places at approximately the same time: in her room, atop the tower, and
departing to buy spell components. The sightings proved contradiction, not the fate of the real
wizard. Sister Pira used the damaged crystal ball to seek the fragment broken from it, receiving a
bearing southeast—broadly toward Trammel Town, though the direction could lead elsewhere, and might
be a trail deliberately laid to deceive.

Klaus, Sandor's devoted bloodhound, followed the robe's scent to an empty berth in carriage number
twelve. That carriage had left hours earlier for Trammel Town under papers apparently authorized by
Captain Blueberry and Maria herself. The authorization was convincing. It was also false. Whether
the missing people actually travelled in it, or whether the carriage was only bait, remained
unproven.

The scale of the disappearance made certainty still more dangerous. Forty-eight people had gone
missing one by one over barely more than a day: guards, servants, wizards, Captain Blueberry, and
others who had seemingly left for ordinary errands, supplies, or private visits and never returned.
The party chose not to declare them traitors merely because they were absent. They began building a
verified roster, screening those who remained, reconstructing movements through the castle, and
keeping enough guards at their posts that the cure would not become another vulnerability.

Sebastian Argras, the Rannek seneschal, returned exhausted, dazed, and unable at first to explain
where he had been. Angel's *lesser restoration* removed exhaustion and a weak charm. His last clear
memory was of speaking with Kethbjorn, making that wizard a possible lead without making him guilty.
Sebastian then set about organizing the servants, screening them through people already verified,
and helping restore some structure to a household that had been taught to doubt itself.

The party also faced the remains of the false Sandor from the throne room. Beneath the stolen face
had been Face-Stealer-like anatomy, but the corpse had not reverted as an ordinary shapeshifter
should. It appeared instead to be one more of Crispin Mallow's constructions: copied flesh,
necromancy or grafting, and a victim kept visible enough that terror could do part of the work for
him.

Mallow's purpose has not yet been fully proved, but the pattern grows clearer. Oren Bell's account
placed Angel's shards of the Emerald Shield of Spring among Mallow's assigned objectives. Yet Mallow
also seems preoccupied with Face-Stealers, changelings, stolen bodies, and the strange materials in
his “legendary collections.” He had already acquired part of an Urdefolcin's heart. The party
suspected that he was preparing something more elaborate—and that his escalating cruelty might mean
either confidence or instability.

The threat extended beyond the castle. Uther Rannek's authentication codes had been compromised, and
Maria could not believe he would knowingly recall Lance while leaving the castle exposed. The party
would not declare Uther replaced or guilty without proof. But either the recall had been forged, or
Uther had already been captured, altered, or otherwise compromised when it was sent.

Meanwhile, Andrew and Sapphire had continued the quieter half of the war: reconnaissance under the
cover of the Taminok family brewery. With Thilas, Airur, Tormil, and James, they took merchant
papers, uniforms, a marked carriage, and a credible reason to enter inns where soldiers and
villagers might speak more freely than they would before an armed patrol.

At the brewery, Sapphire met Dandelion—Dandy—a small fey who knew Feywild magic when he saw it. The
pixies of the fields apparently accepted beer in exchange for their blessing, though Dandy's stories
of the Great Tree and the Water of Life remained folklore inherited from his grandfather, not
settled cosmology. Andrew's family gave the party cover, supplies, and entirely too much
encouragement about whether Andrew and Sapphire ought to become more than colleagues. Neither let
that become the mission. Both understood that danger lay ahead, and Andrew promised Sapphire help if
the “bad day” she feared arrived.

On the following day, the reconnaissance team reached Kurtis and began using the inn as it had
intended: a place to listen. Sapphire, Andrew, Airur, and James went inside while Thilas and Tormil
were left to watch the brewery carriage. There they found a countryside strained toward fracture.
The Hicklanders' revolt is fed by brutal labor conditions, taxes, and wartime hardship. Rumors
suggest that outside hands—perhaps Reformists—have supplied explosives to sharpen grievance into
war.

Kurtis also offered signs that local confidence in Rannekford is failing. Some people believe the
Maltreks may be the stronger power to follow. Others speak of Lord Lance as captured by the
Hicklanders, though that remains public report rather than certainty. A mercenary company calling
itself the Band of the Ladle appeared willing to seek work in the spreading crisis, and its members
heard enough of the Rannek name to hope that rescue, war, and payment might soon arrive together.

The travelers bound for Maiden's Field made their own hard choice. Hellion Blanchimontt, Sir Erik
Malmann, Oren Bell, and Klaus continued under cover toward Maiden's Field rather than pursue the
Trammel Town lead directly. They carried the Marchioness's papers and recognition codes, seeking to
learn whether Lord Lance and Marquess Uther survived, what became of their soldiers, and whether the
March can still be held together before fear becomes defection.

Sister Pira's warning, damaged and incomplete as it was, suggested trouble along the roads eastward
and helped drive that redirection. The party knows there may be danger both at Maiden's Field and
back at Castle Rannek. It does not yet know which front Mallow—or whoever is directing this
operation—means to force them to abandon.

Nor is Mallow's work confined to stolen faces. Reports now link him to coerced Face-Stealers and
changelings, to fragments of an Urdefolcin heart, and to creatures altered with dragon or chimera
anatomy. Stacy has reportedly been taken toward a moon well or healing pool that Sister Pira may be
able to activate. If that account is true, it offers hope—but not yet a cure.

So House Rannek stands, but it does not stand secure. The castle counts its missing. The roads carry
forged authority. Maiden's Field smolders beneath revolt and rumor. And the party has divided not
because any road is safe, but because too many people may already be lost to wait for a single,
perfect answer.

Ahead lies Maiden's Field—where the carriage bears House Rannek's mark, the truth of Lance and
Uther may finally be waiting, and every stranger has had time to learn what that mark is worth.
