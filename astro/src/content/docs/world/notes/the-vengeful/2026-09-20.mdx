## Recess in the Garden

- The formal inquiry entered recess while Oren pursued an unidentified person toward the castle
  exterior.
  - He saw no one else and promised to report when he learned more.
- Angel escorted Crispin Mallow inside for pudding, then returned with him to the garden.
  - A cleaning lady recognized Mallow's name but did not explain why.
  - Sir Brastok watched from a distance.
- Crispin described surviving through begging or finding food and reacted uneasily when Angel asked
  about a suspicious man resembling one of his uncles.
  - He could not explain how he had reached the castle and feared that he might have been sold.
- Angel told Crispin that he had once been a senior Reformist Church officer responsible for grave
  harm.
  - She said he had trapped a dragon's soul inside himself.
  - According to Angel, her celestial power freed him from corrupting influences and released the
    dragon.
  - She used the dragon's boon to turn him into a ten-year-old boy without his adult memories.
- Crispin remained skeptical, so Angel showed him her spellbook and the current date as evidence.
  - She acknowledged that her companions had wanted to kill his former self.
  - She refused because he was now a child and because she had promised the dragon to care for him.
- Crispin disclosed that he had killed his mother while she was strangling him and calling him a
  monster.
  - Angel characterized the killing as self-defense and suggested that an outside influence may have
    changed his formerly kind mother.
  - Crispin said his father blamed him for driving her insane.
- Angel rejected the idea that Crispin's past required his execution.
  - She promised to protect him and urged him to choose a different future.
  - Most castle servants did not know his identity; Hellion and Angel's parents were among the few
    who did.

## Fragments of Crispin's Past

- Angel arranged to hide Crispin at the castle under the story that she had killed or otherwise
  dealt with him.
  - Crispin remembered distrusting the Maidenfelds and tentatively associated Tristan Maidenfeld
    with an uncle or relative.
  - He preferred not to meet people from his former life, though registries or elderly acquaintances
    might identify his parents.
- Crispin recognized the symbol on an emblem Angel had received from the crown prince.
  - His mother had displayed the same symbol on a treasured dress.
  - This suggested an uncertain connection to the Purple Dragonhearts or another organization using
    that emblem.
- Angel and Crispin considered asking elves for help.
  - Hellion was available as an elven contact, while Oren Bell had once
    worked with the Reformist Church.
- Angel privately showed Crispin an issue of Legally Bare and disclosed that she modeled for it.
  - Crispin worried that its young models might be coerced, recalling an aunt who made children pose
    for buyers.
  - Angel maintained that the models participated voluntarily and believed concealed magical signals
    in the magazine helped exploited girls escape, though she could not demonstrate that claim.
- Angel described the Reformist Church as abducting gifted children, killing protectors,
  brainwashing captives, trafficking victims, and harvesting organs.
  - She said Miya Campbell had helped free people in Eastonton and that the party possessed evidence
    against the Church.
- Angel gave Crispin a dragon scale and described the dragon's offer to turn him into an infant.
  - She also mentioned Minfilia, a teenage necromancer associated with animated skeletons.
- Crispin wanted to garden but feared unfamiliar adults, so Angel agreed that he could accompany her
  when she traveled.
  - Flooding had destroyed crops, and Rannek reportedly had only about three days of food remaining.
  - The group expected to need druidic help.
- Hellion and another companion privately reviewed Crispin's transformation and rejected killing
  him.
  - A nearby worker and several birds were the only apparent eavesdroppers.
- The companions shared Crispin's distrust of the Maidenfelds.
  - The Baron's polished answers and recognition of spoken Draconic suggested a possible draconic
    connection.
  - The group questioned why the family protected its own waters while neighboring people went
    hungry.

## The Interrupted Conference

- Before the conference, the companions theorized that the Maidenfelds had deliberately allowed
  Mallow to capture a ship and submarine.
  - They suspected a plot to incapacitate the Marquis and manipulate the succession, possibly
    through a doppelganger.
  - Merlin had renounced his position, leaving Lance as a possible successor whom the plotters may
    have expected to die.
  - The group decided to reserve judgment until after the meeting.
- The companions also discussed investigating an elven outreach effort despite Oren's ban and the
  possibility that Crispin would also be unwelcome.
- Oren withdrew to a quiet southern garden to meditate, while the group prepared for the conference.
  - The gathering included Angel's family, companions, and arriving nobles.
  - A missed summons left them without the intended ready-room preparations or change of clothing.
- While waiting, the companions discussed the unpaid mercenaries, Kelthyr's condition, and several
  celestial acquaintances.
  - Angel's mother had offered to cover the remaining payment and add a bonus.
- The Baron arrived without Tristan and asked to postpone the conference because Steven Maidenfeld
  was missing.
  - Steven had teleported away after being summoned by his adjutant to address a fleet emergency.
  - Steven, his adjutant, and the entire destination ship then vanished and resisted ordinary and
    magical detection.
- The party agreed to incorporate Steven's disappearance into its existing investigation.
  - A paired <Item name="sending stone" src="dmg" /> or another personal effect might assist.
  - <Spell name="locate creature" src="phb" /> was judged unreliable across the sea and whatever
    interference concealed the vessel.
  - Even the ships' magical navigation instruments returned nothing.
- The group considered using Steven or the Baron as an anchor for <Spell name="teleport" src="phb" />.
  - The destination might be underwater, extraplanar, warded, or otherwise hazardous.
  - The Baron appeared deeply uncomfortable with being used as the anchor.
  - A teleportation scroll was available, but the ward and uncertain restrictions beyond the local
    waters made immediate use dangerous.

## The Secret of the Haven

- Angel prepared to teleport by drinking a cerulean potion that granted water breathing and stronger
  swimming.
  - The Baron stressed that a pocket dimension was only one hypothesis and suggested anti-magic,
    planar separation, or an emergency as alternatives.
- Instead of teleporting immediately, Angel cast <Spell name="legend lore" src="phb" /> on Steven.
  - Warmth, light, and ethereal wings manifested around her.
  - The revelation established that Steven was alive, endangered, and coerced.
  - He was in a place of obscure legend that had remained hidden for centuries and was preserved by
    an unidentified line.
- The companions persuaded the family to allow an expanded investigation.
  - In a private ready-room discussion, the Maidenfelds disclosed a possible fifth fae-blessed place
    beyond the four commonly known.
  - Its protections followed both Maidenfeld blood and name and were connected to the Dragonheart
    line.
- The hidden place technically lay within Senera but occupied its own stable
  realm.
  - Entry required a particular underwater approach.
  - Submarines concealed and simplified the route, but a knowledgeable swimmer capable of breathing
    underwater could follow it.
  - Magical protections prevented teleportation from bypassing the approach.
- The family's westbound trade and ships helped disguise the destination.
  - The site lacked merfolk or obvious physical defenders, making secrecy especially important.
  - A local magical source had allowed Maidenfeld spellcasters to practice during the magicless era,
    but the family denied that it was one of the known orbs of power.
- The realm was a dragon sanctuary called the Haven.
  - It was not part of the Feywild and may have been created by fey folk from the Realm Bridge.
  - It contained unhatched chromatic dragon eggs but no living dragons.
  - Their caretakers feared both hatching and destroying them.
- The party debated whether chromatic dragons were inherently dangerous or could be shaped by their
  upbringing.
  - Auraxx, described as the last known metallic dragon, might have been capable of guiding the
    hatchlings, but was also said to have become a victim of Mallow.
- The group warned that the Reformist Church could exploit the eggs.
  - The <Item name="orb of dragonkind" src="dmg" />, attributed to Tiamat, might allow an evil
    spellcaster to hatch and command them.
- Attention shifted to Crispin Mallow's missing journal.
  - Marquis Uther Rannek remembered his captor repeatedly reading and writing in it during his
    captivity in a basement beneath a dilapidated cottage in a Maiden's Field village.
  - The group proposed searching the site and consulting a nearby hag coven.
  - The rescued elves and other captives remained under care pending repatriation.

## Return to the Burned Settlement

- The party prioritized Mallow's journal as evidence of what he had discovered, communicated to the
  Reformist Church, and learned about the Haven.
  - The MS Paprika remained undisturbed under guard.
  - Another force continued the captive-rescue and people-smuggling investigation.
- Angel, the Marquis, Crispin, and several companions departed for a burned settlement near Maiden's
  Field.
  - The destination lay about a week from Rannekford, so the group used a familiar former home as
    the conduit for <Spell name="teleport" src="phb" />.
  - The spell landed them on target in an overgrown field surrounded by mud, broken dikes,
    ash-covered ruins, and a single dilapidated house.
- Hellion carried the party across the quagmire to the likely basement ruins.
- Angel cast <Spell name="detect magic" src="phb" /> but initially found no unexpected magic.
  - She then used <Spell name="speak with animals" src="phb" /> and bread to question a squirrel.
  - The squirrel indicated a burrow-like entrance blocked by a large stone.
- The rubble had been arranged deliberately to conceal the entrance.
  - The party moved the slab and uncovered a rough underground complex.
  - Crispin began shaking with fear, so Angel stayed outside with him while the others descended.
- The explorers found hurriedly excavated passages and a long-dead body preserved by nonmagical
  chemicals.
  - The corpse was Crispin's father and resembled Crispin's former adult appearance.
  - A coin called a dragon had been stitched inside its mouth, supposedly to purchase the soul's
    silence; a companion removed it.
- The Marquis recognized the chamber from his captivity and recalled being used as bait because
  Angel was somehow necessary.
  - He remembered the Reformist Church repeatedly coordinating with his captor.
  - The party considered using <Spell name="encode thoughts" src="ggr" /> or similar magic to
    recover conversations spoken in an unidentified language.
- The laboratory contained preserved anatomical specimens and concealed materials visible through
  true sight.
  - These included spectral matter, a desiccated will-o'-wisp, shapeshifter skin, and material
    resembling displacer-beast hide.
- A magical object glowed inside a taxidermied squirrel.
  - The party detected an evocation trap and removed it with a <Spell name="dispel magic" src="phb" />
    rune before opening the seams.

## The Dragon-Heart Box

- The taxidermied squirrel contained a decoder-wheel-like device linked to a message.
  - The group retained it because only the Duchess was believed capable of operating it.
- True sight exposed another illusion sustained by an item and concealing a many-colored magical
  box.
  - Inspection suggested that forcing it open with <Spell name="dispel magic" src="phb" /> might
    destroy its contents.
  - The group carried it outside for safer examination.
- The chamber also held an elaborate dragon-heart dress.
  - Angel folded it carefully and stored it in the <Item name="bag of holding" src="dmg" /> Minfilia
    had given her.
  - The dress and the dragon-heart coin suggested a link between Crispin's mother and the Maidenfeld
    or Dragonheart lines.
- The box recognized Crispin through divination magic but required several conditions to open.
  - Its recess reshaped itself around the purple dragon-heart coin.
  - It reacted to Crispin's hand and still demanded a spoken or sung component.
- The group tested favorite words, family references, the dress, dancing, humming, and several songs
  without success.
  - Investigation suggested that the correct song had been identified but that Crispin himself
    needed to perform it under another qualifying condition.
- Crispin recalled frightened children passing through the settlement and an uncle coming there to
  buy.
  - He feared the inhabitants may have trafficked children.
  - He also described dreams in which he became a red dragon and burned the settlement, while
    clearly distinguishing those dreams from memory.
- Recovered or shared memories contained court wizards, a false Gwyniviere associated with a
  Face-Stealer, and a bargain involving a child.
  - Crispin remembered that Oren Bell knew a phrase capable of deactivating certain constructs or
    creatures, but he could not recover it.
- As Crispin repeatedly sang his mother's lullaby,
  [Flight of Dragons](../../novelty/flight-of-dragons.mdx), its words shifted into Draconic
  snarls and roars.
  - Angel recognized that he could produce Draconic without knowingly understanding it.
  - Hellion translated the song as Flight of Dragons.
- Crispin held the coin against the box and sang Flight of Dragons in his distinctive,
  imperfect Draconic.
  - His particular pronunciation proved essential.
  - The box opened and revealed an intricate leather-bound book.

## The Concealed Journal

- Crispin's physical contact caused magically hidden writing to appear in the leather-bound journal.
  - Even touching it with his feet activated the writing.
  - Once revealed, other people could read it.
- The writer claimed that his plans were nearing completion and denounced another bloodline as
  false.
  - He intended to reveal the rightful king of Senera.
  - A child guided by local spirits had found two emerald shards.
- The writer believed a curse ran through his veins and that celestial blood plus a final sacrifice
  could purge it.
  - The group concluded that Angel was the intended source of celestial blood.
- Entries from the writer's youth described incorporating pieces of other people into his body.
  - He treated the Reformist Church and those who admired his power as tools.
  - He also described the Reformists as strangely familiar, like returning to the home he had
    burned.
- The journal's writer claimed responsibility for burning the settlement.
  - It claimed his mother descended from an expelled Maidenfeld cadet branch that had retained ties
    to the Dragonhearts.
  - He blamed the recognized Maidenfeld line for stripping his branch of its property and status.
  - The group identified his secret language as Draconic and interpreted his ambitions as a claim to
    a Dragonheart birthright.
- The writer's parents were tentatively identified as Lalandra and Mallow, suggesting that archival
  or marriage records could verify the lineage.
- The journal described capturing a Maidenfeld captain and turning him into a puppet to redirect a
  prototype submarine.
  - The puppet was eventually discovered and destroyed, but the writer retained the vessel.
- The writer documented extensive abuses and experiments.
  - He planned to dissect Sister Rachel and replace his liver with hers.
  - He experimented on living and dead elves while studying their longevity.
  - He developed a method intended to keep Face-Stealers and Changelings from reverting after death
    by erasing their original identities.
- Hags recognized both a curse and an unwritable bargain in the writer's blood.
  - Every attempt to record the bargain's terms produced only the statement that he had made a deal.
- The journal discussed Minfilia's ability to preserve magic in undead bodies and preparations made
  in Malthrek before traveling toward the Rannic Marches.
- The writer described torturing Marquis Uther Rannek and threatening his family.
  - He intended to sacrifice Uther's youngest daughter, Angel, rather than use her heart for his
    creations as he proposed doing with the other family members.
- Dark archons sent to Bastion reportedly considered promoting the writer into their order.
- A magical page finder highlighted relevant names and targets.
  - The journal showed that the writer had spent years provoking anger in the Hicklands by sending
    threaded puppets disguised as Maidenfeld forces to raid villages.
  - He sought the crown prince's topaz sword and planned another move involving Merlion.
- The writer suspected that the Maidenfelds hid dragon eggs.
  - His suspicions strengthened after he incorporated an entity whose name may have been Auraxx.
- Hellion praised the writer's patience and planning but warned that he had become like the people
  he once hated.
  - Hellion retained the journal as evidence and proposed producing a readable translation later.

## Boundaries

- Steven's survival, danger, and coercion are established by Angel's divination, but his exact
  location and captor remain unresolved.
- The Haven, its underwater approach, and its unhatched chromatic dragon eggs are treated as
  disclosed facts; its creator, power source, and complete protections remain uncertain.
- The table discussion of Tiphanie's Orb of Dragonkind and dragon-rallying plans was not an
  in-character disclosure; Hellion explicitly did not know she possessed the orb.
- Crispin's present child identity is distinct from the deeds attributed to his former adult self;
  the notes do not assign moral responsibility for forgotten adult actions to the child as a settled
  conclusion.
- Crispin's mother's draconic nature, Crispin's own possible dragon ancestry, and the identity of
  the entity incorporated by his former self remain theories.
- Claims recorded in the journal represent the writer's account and require corroboration where they
  concern lineage, bargains, kingship, historical events, or successful experiments.
- The Draco-Giant War history, casualty count, and associated proper names come from an in-world
  book discussion and are not independently verified.
- No uncertainly transcribed place, person, faction, spell, or item name has been promoted to
  settled canon.
- Private speaker identities and player-to-character mappings are excluded; uncertain in-world
  attribution is retained without exposing private identities.
