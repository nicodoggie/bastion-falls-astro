## Recess in the Garden

- The formal inquiry entered recess while Oren pursued Tristan Maidenfeld toward the castle
  exterior after his activity inside a cone of silence.
  - Tristan did not appear to be fleeing Oren; his destination and purpose remained unknown.
  - Oren saw no one else and promised to report when he learned more.
- Angel escorted Crispin Mallow inside for pudding, then returned with him to the garden.
  - A cleaning lady found Mallow's name familiar but did not identify him.
  - Sir Brastok watched from a distance; Angel later explained that he was her personal guard.
- Crispin described surviving through begging or finding food and reacted uneasily when Angel asked
  about Tristan, whom Crispin thought resembled one of his uncles.
  - He could not explain how he had reached the castle and feared that he might have been sold.
- Angel told Crispin that he had once been a senior Reformist Church officer responsible for grave
  harm.
  - She said he had trapped a dragon's soul inside himself.
  - According to Angel, her celestial power freed him from corrupting influences and released the
    dragon.
  - She used the dragon's boon to turn him into a ten-year-old boy without his adult memories.
- Crispin remained skeptical, so Angel showed him her spellbook and the current date as evidence.
  - She acknowledged that her companions had wanted to kill his former self.
  - She refused because he was now a child and because she had promised the dragon to care for him.
- Crispin disclosed that he had killed his mother while she was strangling him and calling him a
  monster.
  - Angel characterized the killing as self-defense and suggested that an outside influence may have
    changed his formerly kind mother.
  - Crispin said his father blamed him for driving her insane.
- Angel rejected the idea that Crispin's past required his execution.
  - She promised to protect him and urged him to choose a different future.
  - Most castle servants did not know his identity; Hellion and Angel's parents were among the few
    who did.

## The Secret of the Haven

- Hellion produced a cerulean potion described as granting water breathing and swimming, and
  instructed Angel to drink it before attempting teleportation. Its consumption was not established.
  - He hoped Angel might pass the suspected ward because she was not an elf; this was a proposed
    workaround, not a demonstrated exemption.
  - The Baron stressed that a pocket dimension was only one hypothesis and suggested anti-magic,
    planar separation, or an emergency as alternatives.
- Instead of teleporting immediately, Angel cast <Spell name="legend lore" src="phb" /> on Steven.
  - Warmth, light, and ethereal wings manifested around her.
  - She spoke the revelation aloud in a changed voice, as though someone else were speaking
    through her; the brightness faded afterward.
  - The revelation established that Steven was alive, endangered, and coerced.
  - He was in a place of obscure legend that had remained hidden for centuries and was preserved by
    an unidentified line.
- The companions persuaded the family to allow an expanded investigation.
  - They offered to preserve proprietary secrets provided these posed no threat to Senera.
  - In a private ready-room discussion, the Baron disclosed a fifth fae-blessed place beyond the
    four commonly known, which he believed was Steven's likely location.
  - Robert was excluded from the discussion; the Baron said his responsibilities were accounts
    and that he would be told what he needed to know afterward.
  - The Baron said its protections depended on both Maidenfeld blood and name. Ownership by a
    different house would lose those protections, despite the Dragonhearts' shared ancestry.
- The Baron described the hidden place as a stable, demiplane-like realm attached to a particular
  location within Senera, rather than part of the Feywild.
  - Entry required a particular underwater approach.
  - The interior itself was not underwater, and elves could enter because it counted as part of
    Senera.
  - Submarines concealed and simplified the route, but a knowledgeable swimmer capable of breathing
    underwater could follow it.
  - The Baron warned that teleporting there would leave the traveler lost, even if familiar with
    the destination; the underwater approach could not safely be bypassed that way.
- The family's westbound trade and ships helped disguise the destination, but the Baron stressed
  that real trade with western cultures also took place.
  - The site lacked merfolk or obvious physical defenders, making secrecy especially important.
  - A local magical source had allowed Maidenfeld spellcasters to practice during the magicless era,
    but the family denied that it was one of the known orbs of power.
- The realm was a dragon sanctuary called the Haven.
  - It was not part of the Feywild and may have been created by fey folk from the Realm Bridge.
  - It contained unhatched chromatic dragon eggs but no living dragons.
  - Their caretakers feared both hatching and destroying them.
- The party debated whether chromatic dragons were inherently dangerous or could be shaped by their
  upbringing.
  - Auraxx, described as the last known metallic dragon, might have been capable of guiding the
    hatchlings, but was also said to have become a victim of Mallow.
  - The Baron said his family's records contained no sightings of metallic dragons in Senera,
    only stories from other fey folk; Auraxx's name was new to him.
  - Hellion argued that upbringing could change chromatic dragons, citing Feywild examples.
    The Baron did not accept those examples as proof of what would happen in Senera.
- The group warned that the Reformist Church could exploit the eggs.
  - The <Item name="orb of dragonkind" src="dmg" />, attributed to Tiamat, might allow an evil
    spellcaster to hatch and command them.
  - Hellion warned that the artifact could force the dragons out of hiding; whether the Reformists
    possessed it or knew the Haven's secret remained unresolved.
  - The suggestion that Mallow's incorporated dragon could sense other dragons was challenged:
    the Baron said such a sense had been written about but not confirmed.
- Attention shifted to Crispin Mallow's missing journal.
  - Marquis Uther Rannek remembered his captor repeatedly reading and writing in it during his
    captivity in a basement beneath a dilapidated cottage in a Maiden's Field village.
  - The group proposed searching the site and consulting a nearby hag coven.
  - The rescued elves and other captives remained under care pending repatriation.

## The Concealed Journal

- The book was the journal of Crispin's former adult self. His physical contact caused its magically
  hidden writing to appear.
  - Even touching it with his feet activated the writing.
  - Once revealed, other people could read it.
- Mallow claimed that his plans were nearing completion and denounced another bloodline as
  false.
  - He intended to reveal the rightful king of Senera.
  - Earlier entries discussed Angel finding two emerald shards, guided by the spirits of Senera.
- Mallow believed a curse ran through his veins and that celestial blood plus a final sacrifice
  could purge it.
  - The journal established that Angel was the intended sacrifice and source of celestial blood.
- Entries from Mallow's youth described incorporating pieces of other people into his body.
  - He treated the Reformist Church and those who admired his power as tools.
  - He also described the Reformists as strangely familiar, like returning to the home he had
    burned.
- The journal revealed that Mallow had burned Green Barrel, overturning the earlier attribution
  to bandits in the local magistrates' official record; the group explicitly confirmed his
  responsibility.
  - The journal claimed his mother descended from an expelled Maidenfeld cadet branch with ties
    to the Dragonhearts.
  - He blamed the recognized Maidenfeld line for stripping his branch of its property and status.
  - The group identified his secret language as Draconic and interpreted his ambitions as a claim to
    a Dragonheart birthright.
  - The child Crispin questioned whether his adult self had imagined that ancestry; Angel proposed
    checking the records rather than treating the journal as proof.
- Crispin identified his parents as Lilandra and Tempo Mallow; archival or marriage records might
  verify the lineage claimed in the journal.
- The journal described capturing a Maidenfeld captain and turning him into a puppet to redirect a
  prototype submarine.
  - The submarine was a decommissioned prototype awaiting destruction. Mallow used the captain to
    redirect it to another stretch of coast.
  - The puppet was eventually discovered and destroyed, but Mallow retained the vessel.
- Mallow documented extensive abuses and experiments.
  - He planned to dissect Sister Rachel and replace his liver with hers.
  - He experimented on living and dead elves while studying their longevity.
    He also used the bodies of elves killed when attempts to smuggle them out of Senera forced them
    against the boundary and ships' hulls; he concluded longevity was not solely anatomical.
  - Hellion privately read entries on Face-Stealers and Changelings. Mallow claimed success with a
    Face-Stealer method: forcing them to take more faces until their original identity was erased,
    then imprinting a final face that persisted after death. The entries did not identify suppliers.
- Mallow recorded that hags recognized a curse and a deal in his blood, which he took as
  confirmation of Maidenfeld ancestry and its curse and boon.
  - The children of the crone offered him a deal whose terms he could not write.
  - Every attempt to record the bargain's terms produced only the statement that he had made a deal.
- The journal discussed Minfilia's ability to preserve magic in undead bodies and preparations made
  in Malthrek before traveling toward the Rannic Marches.
  - Mallow wanted to learn more about her necromancy, but did not expect their paths to cross unless
    she came to the Rannek Marches. This did not establish collaboration between them.
- Mallow described torturing Marquis Uther Rannek and threatening his family.
  - He intended to sacrifice Uther's youngest daughter, Angel, rather than use her heart for his
    creations as he proposed doing with the other family members.
- Dark archons sent to Bastion reportedly considered promoting Mallow into their order.
- A magical page finder highlighted relevant names and targets.
  - Helena Cooper was not mentioned in the journal. Mallow's apparent ignorance concerning her
    was an inference: she had remained unharmed despite having been in direct contact with him.
    Neither her omission nor her survival established what he actually knew.
  - The review placed Mallow's work with the local clergy in Malthrek within roughly the past seven
    to ten years, rather than at the time of Duke Ellington's initial conversion.
  - The journal showed that Mallow had spent years provoking anger in the Hicklands by sending
    threaded puppets disguised as Maidenfeld forces to raid villages.
  - He planned to deal with the crown after securing the Maidenfelds. The journal's only specific
    reference to the crown prince concerned how to take his topaz sword.
- Hellion privately read Mallow's suspicions that the Maidenfelds hid dragon eggs and that their
  secret involved dragons; Mallow still intended to discover exactly what was there.
  - His suspicions strengthened after he incorporated Auraxx into his body.
- Hellion praised Mallow's patience and planning but warned that he had become like the people
  he once hated.
  - Hellion retained the journal as evidence and proposed producing a readable translation later.
  - The child Crispin could speak Draconic but could not read the journal, even when its writing was
    visible. Hellion offered to teach him.

## Boundaries

- The lockbox is connected to House Dragonheart; the family name is not a literal dragon-heart
  description. Its connection does not by itself establish Crispin's claimed lineage.
- The preserved corpse in Green Barrel is Tempo Mallow, as confirmed outside play. The explorers
  initially inferred that it was Crispin's father from its resemblance to his former adult self;
  that inference is distinct from an explicit identification by Crispin or a journal entry.
- Steven's survival, danger, and coercion are established by Angel's divination, but his exact
  location and captor remain unresolved.
- The Haven, its underwater approach, and its unhatched chromatic dragon eggs are treated as
  disclosed facts; its creator, power source, and complete protections remain uncertain.
- The table discussion of Tiphanie's Orb of Dragonkind and dragon-rallying plans was not an
  in-character disclosure; Hellion explicitly did not know she possessed the orb.
- Crispin's present child identity is distinct from the deeds attributed to his former adult self;
  the notes do not assign moral responsibility for forgotten adult actions to the child as a settled
  conclusion.
- Crispin's mother's draconic nature and Crispin's own possible dragon ancestry remain theories;
  Auraxx's incorporation is distinct from a claim of inherited dragon ancestry.
- Hellion did not read the Face-Stealer method or the final dragon-related entries aloud; their
  contents are not treated as a disclosure to every companion.
- The recovered leather-bound book is Mallow's journal, and the box's successful opening sequence
  is established. The box's maker and imprinting history remain unknown; the journal did not
  actually burn in this scene. Hellion's destruction story was a deliberate deception.

  remembered child bargain was the same agreement.
- The fleet recall, Jessica's assignment, and the independent approach to the Haven were announced
  or proposed, not shown completed in this session.
- Claims recorded in the journal represent Mallow's account and require corroboration where they
  concern lineage, bargains, kingship, historical events, or successful experiments.
  This does not reopen settled facts such as his authorship, his parents' names, or his burning of
  Green Barrel; the magistrates' official bandit attribution is distinct from actual responsibility.

  book discussion and are not independently verified.
- No uncertainly transcribed place, person, faction, spell, or item name has been promoted to
  settled canon.
- Private speaker identities and player-to-character mappings are excluded; uncertain in-world
  attribution is retained without exposing private identities.
