The **Biscuit Reliquary of Stolen Narratology** is a battered blue Dutch cookie tin that has long
outlived any honest relationship with biscuits. However carefully it is emptied, it soon resumes
rattling with sewing needles, buttons, thread spools, hooks, and tiny brass thimbles.

The reliquary steals specialized words rather than meanings. A victim remembers the concept clearly
and may describe it at tiresome length, but the precise term itself remains missing, suspended in
the mind as a permanent almost-remembrance. Scholars, priests, lawyers, wizards, taxonomists, and
others whose authority depends on exact vocabulary are especially vulnerable to its humiliations.

Recovered accounts describe stolen words nesting inside the tin's ordinary contents. One word might
be wound around mustard-colored thread; another might catch in the eye of a bent needle or rest
beneath a cracked mother-of-pearl button. Choosing wrongly can loosen another word from the seeker's
mind, which has made several attempted recoveries worse than the original affliction.

The reliquary is bound by the Principle of Prior Emptiness: it cannot steal any cognate, descendant,
borrowed form, or recognizable household cousin of a word for cookie in any language. Domestic
philosophers explain this by noting that a Dutch cookie tin never contains cookies. The cookies are
therefore already absent, and absence cannot be stolen twice.

> “No cookie may be stolen from the tin, for no cookie was ever in the tin. This is the first law
> of grandmotherly storage.”
>
> — attributed to Sister Hanneke of the Blue Thread
