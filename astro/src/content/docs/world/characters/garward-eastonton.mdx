**Garward Eastonton** is a Seneran nobleman, paladin, and head of the
[Eastonton](../families/eastonton.mdx) family. He is known publicly as the Viscount of
[Eastonton](../locations/eastonton.mdx), a veteran of the
[Poasan-Apgarian Conflict](../events/poasan-apgarian-conflict.mdx), and Lord Commander of the
[Eastonton Paladinate][brigade].
Recent investigations into the Eastonton Paladinate have also placed him at the center of several
unresolved allegations concerning
[Reformist Divine Masochism](../religions/divine-masochism/reformist/index.mdx), aberrant
parasites, and the misuse of Paladinate authority.

[brigade]: ../organizations/the-knightly-order-of-the-angel-of-justice---eastonton-brigade.mdx

## Overview

Garward is regarded in official Eastonton accounts as one of
[Senera](../locations/senera/index.mdx)'s heroes of the Poasan-Apgarian Conflict. His wartime
service elevated both his personal reputation and the standing of his house, binding the Eastonton
name more closely to the militant prestige of the Angel of Justice. In later years he held both
secular and religious authority in the northern Inespell Marches: as viscount he stood at the head
of the territorial household, and as Lord Commander he commanded the most important Orthodox
military institution in Eastonton.

This union of noble authority and Paladinate command made Garward unusually difficult to oppose.
Those who served him were not merely tenants, servants, or soldiers, but often men and women whose
social, religious, and military obligations all converged on his person. The same concentration of
authority later complicated inquiries into the Paladinate, since witnesses had to distinguish
ordinary obedience from magical coercion, religious pressure, parasite influence, and deliberate
conspiracy.

## Public Career

Garward's father is said to have sent him into the Paladinate in an attempt to curb his arrogance.
The decision instead placed Garward in the institution that would make him famous. By the end of the
Poasan-Apgarian Conflict, he had become a celebrated figure whose reputation for courage and command
continued to shape Eastonton politics long after the war.

As Lord Commander, Garward presided over the Eastonton Brigade of the Angel of Justice. The
Brigade's public chapel, barracks, mess hall, and internal hierarchy maintained the appearance of an
[Orthodox Divine Masochist](../religions/divine-masochism/orthodox/index.mdx) institution, even as
later infiltrators reported signs of hidden marking, suspicious magical residues, and senior
paladins whose private conduct did not match the Order's discipline.

## Family and Household

Garward was married to [Maude Eastonton](maude-eastonton.mdx) and was the father of Gareth
Eastonton, his eldest son, [Aigral Eastonton](aigral-eastonton.mdx), and
[Nandor Eastonton](nandor-eastonton.mdx). Maude died within a year of the family's return from the
Poasan-Apgarian Conflict. His siblings included [Boyle Eastonton](boyle-eastonton.mdx) and
[Nelson Eastonton](nelson-eastonton.mdx), and his mother was Natalia Eastonton, Viscountess Dowager
of Eastonton. The household's later history was marked by internal estrangement, disputed loyalties,
and the collapse of trust between Garward and Boyle.

Later testimony from Natalia described Garward as a man who desired admiration and exaltation more
than ordinary affection. She also connected this trait to the scandal involving
[Markle Eastonton](markle-eastonton.mdx), Boyle's former wife, who had reportedly loved Garward from
childhood. Garward became involved with Markle despite her marriage to Boyle and despite his own
marriage. Accounts differ on whether the affair was intended as a deliberate insult to Boyle or
whether Garward simply gave little thought to his brother's humiliation.

The family conflict deepened after the death of Boyle and Garward's father. Natalia claimed that
her husband made her promise to remove herself from the struggle against Garward, and that dangerous
associates entered the household soon afterward. In the same account, a parasite was placed in
Natalia's head, leaving her physically weakened and forcing her to lock away parts of her own mind
to survive.

## Personality and Reputation

Garward's admirers presented him as a commanding and charismatic paladin whose authority had been
earned in war. Less flattering accounts describe the same qualities as vanity, hunger for adoration,
and a capacity to convert devotion into personal obedience. Natalia's description of him emphasizes
the latter reading: in her view, the war made Garward a hero and worsened the arrogance his father
had once hoped the Paladinate would discipline.

This reputation also colored later interpretations of his apparent cooperation with hostile forces.
Some witnesses believed Garward was fully responsible for his choices. Others, including Natalia at
moments, questioned whether he acted freely or under Reformist or parasitic influence. The evidence
therefore produced two competing images: Garward as a corrupted commander who used foreign powers
for his own exaltation, and Garward as a powerful host whose own ambition made him especially useful
to forces already capable of altering memory, desire, and obedience.

## Allegations and Evidence

The strongest allegations against Garward concern his possible Reformist allegiance and his role in
the aberrant compromise of Eastonton. During the Eastonton crisis, Miya warned Boyle's allies that
Boyle's brother was dangerous, corrupt, and associated with experiments involving aberrations. Later
reports also alleged that many of the Eastonton paladins had been turned toward the Reformist church
under Garward's influence.

Natalia's testimony remains the most direct family evidence. She stated that Garward returned
about fifteen years before the crisis with an aberrant presence, began feeding it to her, and
prevented her from stopping him by casting _hold person_. She believed she may have been targeted
because of her magical ability. She also later cursed Garward, intending any parasite or invader in
his mind to feel overwhelming fear.

Other evidence came through Henson Eastonton, who served Garward and the Reformists
instrumentally. Henson described Garward's use of memory compartmentalization, with selected
memories encoded into a vessel in the Eastonton Manor court wizard's wing. He also identified
Garward as likely being with other paladins and gave
[Thrantorbury](../locations/thrantorbury.mdx) as a possible lead, while admitting that Garward's
movements and defenses made his exact location uncertain.

Physical and magical clues supported parts of these accusations without fully resolving them.
Infiltrators in the Paladinate found Green Liquid residue and syringes in Sir Essin's hidden room,
with the substance identified as possibly
[Green Liquid](../items/potion-of-aberrant-transformation.mdx) or a Garward Eastonton Green Draught.
The same investigations connected Eastonton to the
[Orb of Absorption](../misc/orbs-of-power/orb-of-absorption.mdx), parasite-bearing captives, and
victims whose magical power was being exploited beneath the Paladinate.

Recovered and reported memories further linked Garward to the Children of the Crone and to plots
involving Aigral and the Ruby Amulet of Fall. Those accounts suggest that Garward communicated with
Henson's group, that he or his allies debated whether Aigral should be killed after the amulet was
taken, and that Reformist agents wanted Aigral to retrieve the
[Ruby Amulet of Fall](../novelty/the-elven-boons.mdx). These memories remain dangerous evidence:
they were filtered through magical extraction, parasite influence, and removed or compartmentalized
recollections.

By the time later intelligence placed Garward in Thrantorbury rather than Eastonton, some allied
reports described him as a Reformist and possibly a high-ranking figure. The same reports credited
him with helping turn Eastonton Paladinate paladins into Reformists. These claims are best treated
as allegations with substantial supporting evidence rather than as a settled public conviction,
since Garward's own condition, the degree of parasite control, and the reliability of altered
memories remain unresolved.

## Encounter Suggestions

Garward can be run as a three-phase paragon encounter. The first phase begins at CR 10, with
Garward fighting in the disciplined public style of the Lord Commander of the Paladinate. The
second phase begins when he drinks the green liquid or loses his first paragon pool, increasing the
pressure to CR 11 while the parasite evolves. The third phase begins when the 1d4-turn countdown
completes or the second paragon pool is destroyed, revealing the full CR 12 paladin mind flayer.

Use the phase transitions as dramatic beats. The first break exposes his Reformist confession
through leaking telepathy and warped divine magic. The second break should feel like the doctrine
has become a body: radiant command, psychic domination, and illithid hunger fused into one
authority.
