**Crispin Mallow**, sometimes styled **Lord Crispin**, is a Seneran fleshcrafter, a displaced member
of [House Dragonheart](../families/dragonheart.mdx), and the Bearer of the Candle within the
[Reformist Church of the Divine Masochist](../religions/divine-masochism/reformist/index.mdx). He
uses harmless borrowed identities to move among households and clergy while studying bodies as
collections of interchangeable parts.

Crispin believes that his humanity is not his natural condition but a seal imprisoning the golden
dragon he considers his true form. His experiments with stolen faces, exchanged limbs, lifelike
flesh golems, and self-reconstruction culminated in the **Gilded Anatomy**, a counterfeit draconic
body assembled within his human shape.

## Dragonheart Displacement

Crispin belongs to a displaced portion of the Dragonheart dynasty, but his exact place in the family
tree and the circumstances of his removal remain obscure. His blood makes him politically useful
without establishing a clear right to succeed the reigning line. This uncertainty became especially
dangerous when Reformist agents sought to discredit the royal family and raised the possibility of
placing a different Dragonheart near the throne.

Whether Crispin is the intended claimant remains unsettled. Reformist leaders may cultivate him as a
sincere alternative to [Marlion I Dragonheart](marlion-i-dragonheart.mdx), or they may merely
preserve his belief because a discarded prince with a constructed destiny is easy to direct. Crispin
himself does not distinguish between dynastic entitlement and draconic apotheosis. To him, both are
evidence that the world has mistaken his temporary shape for his proper station.

## Bearer of the Candle

As Candlebearer, Crispin oversees rites of unveiling: secret vigils, false funerals, staged proofs
of heresy, and the moment when a convert is shown what Reformist teaching claims lies behind
Bastion. His office gives religious purpose to his fascination with concealed structures. A face can
hide another face, a funeral can conceal a living subject, and a human body can conceal the anatomy
he believes it ought to possess.

Like other senior relic-bearers, Crispin wears a cursed infernal piercing. Rather than merely
marking his office, his piercing helps bind together a body repeatedly altered by his own craft. It
anchors borrowed tissue and supports the transformation he interprets as the breaking of his human
seal.

## Fleshcraft

Crispin treats anatomy as material rather than identity. He can reproduce a humanoid face after
studying its owner, remove portions of his own body to act independently, and exchange another
creature's limbs or senses with mismatched replacements. His transformations are physical works of
fleshcraft rather than ordinary illusions, although he reinforces them with wizardry and cultivated
misdirection.

His flesh golems are unusually precise. Where common examples expose the disparate corpses from
which they were assembled, Crispin can construct bodies capable of passing as particular people. He
can also leave these replicas inert until they are needed, allowing them to travel as corpses,
funeral remains, or preserved noble bodies.

## The Gold Canary

When magic disappeared from Senera, an ancient gold dragon escaped the fate suffered by other great
magical creatures by compressing its body, mind, and remaining power into the
<Item name="Gold Canary Figurine of Wondrous Power" src="FTD" />. Its giant-canary shape provided an
economical waking form, while its adult gold dragon shape allowed it to restore its full body during
an emergency. The figurine was therefore a self-made chrysalis rather than a prison.

Crispin brought the dragon out of stasis, killed it, and prevented its body from returning
completely to the figurine. He harvested the manifested dragon for the organs, scales, and magical
structures needed to build his own transformation. The chip in the figurine marks the rupture
through which he severed the dragon from its artifact-body. Its adult-dragon function remains
damaged because portions of that function now survive inside Crispin.

The Gilded Anatomy is the grandest expression of this theft. Gold-dragon scales and organs are bound
to folded human limbs, harvested faces, alchemical supports, and the infernal piercing that prevents
the stolen anatomy from returning to its proper body. The resulting creature possesses authentic
draconic power but is not itself a dragon. It is Crispin's delusion given enough flesh to stand
upright.

Whether the dragon's complete identity survived the murder remains unknown. Returning its harvested
parts to the figurine may permit restoration, but the means and the condition in which the dragon
would awaken have yet to be discovered.

## Castle Malthrek

At [Castle Malthrek](../locations/the-maltreks.mdx), Crispin concealed himself as an outsourced
flower contractor during preparations for the Malthrek funeral rites. He carried chairs, arranged
lilies, repeated distorted rumors from the Travertines, and presented himself as a minor worker
brought in because the usual gardeners were understaffed. The cover placed him close to both the
chapel and the household's preparations for viewing the presumed dead.

His harmless manner did not prevent close observation. Crispin reported suspicious details about
visitors, including unusual attention to a pet and to a disguised visitor's limbs. He was
subsequently identified as the Candlebearer and was later reported away on Reformist business to the
south, with an expected return before the planned festivities.

## Unresolved Operations

Three exceptionally lifelike flesh golems resembling members of House Malthrek were discovered in
ornate caskets near the castle church. Their craftsmanship falls within Crispin's known
capabilities, and their use in false funerals would suit his religious office, but his authorship of
those specific bodies has not been established.

The same uncertainty surrounds the missing Malthrek court wizards. An elderly wizard later appeared
aboard a submarine with his legs or footwear apparently switched, an alteration disturbingly
consistent with Crispin's interest in exchanging body parts. Crispin may be involved with the
missing wizards, the submarines, or their experiments upon captured animals, but those connections
remain under investigation rather than proven extensions of his work.
