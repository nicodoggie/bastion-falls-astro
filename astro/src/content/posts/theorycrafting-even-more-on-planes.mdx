I have been kind of letting the games run without expanding more on the underlying cosmology of
Thera, because frankly, D&D games just run without having to deal with the metaphysical machinery
that underpins it all. Table makes jokes, stories get told, bosses get beat, and the game goes on.
Players too would, inevitably, reach for the standard D&D mechanics and tropes that have persisted
over decades. Stopping that would mean I'd be stopping everyone at the table every 5 minutes just
to basically say "stop having fun!", and they wouldn't even be wrong!

But of course, I just can't help myself from trying to go back and re-explore the deep mysteries of
Thera, and the cosmos around it. This time I have help! Ran and Yomi instrumental in finding some
elements that either needed some additional context, clarification, or things that seemed to just be
missing at all! So they went back and read the past two theorycrafting posts I made and gave me some
very insightful feedback:

  address the first post's periodic cycles

- Do these two posts describe opposing models? Are they refinements? How do they fit together?
- Where does the magic ultimately flow?

In this and the next couple of articles, I plan to expand upon my previous work, and address these

## Chaos

Now, the concept that a chaotic soup of magic was already present in the Glacial Model. Even though
I think it works as is, I think considering the other updates to the greater model, this still has
to be changed, especially since this _is_ the ultimate source of all magic in this cosmological
model.

Previously, I wrote that there are these **Chaotic Planes** where matter cannot exist. They are a
place of raw enegry. It wasn't explicit in my post describing the
[_Glacial Model_](./theorycrafting-more-on-planes.mdx), but I still intended these to be the
_source_ of raw magical energy--the snowpack at the top of the analogical mountain. In the updated
conceptualization, I don't think it makes sense anymore. The reason that I think it doesn't will be
more clear as we continue, but even before that, there is something more fundamentally wrong
conceptually if we model magic from snowmelt.

### Snowpack is not chaotic

Any analogy that fits best with a glacial analog seems, at least to me, like we're describing
potential energy. Something that somehow stores densely packed energy, ready to be released,
supplying the processes downstream. This doesn't _feel_ chaotic to me. Maybe in can be, I'm not
really a physicist or a chaos theorist, but instinctively it doesn't work.

### Primordial Medium

The initial state of the cosmos is a very highly energetic soup of raw magical energy. Very chaotic
interactions, creations and destructions happen randomly in almost every direction. If one were to
"see" into this distant past, one would mainly see bright lights flashing as energetic collisions
constantly annihilate anything that starts to resemble matter. Eventually though, some nucleation
points persist longer. Then some form more stable structures. After long enough, these miniscule
precursors become self-organizing in some way, expanding into a network of material that capture
even more of this raw energy instead of wholly destroying it. I call these
**Proto-Vivial structures**. They are what will become the scaffolding that will support the cycles
that underpin how the cosmos would evolve into its present state.

Maybe this proto-Vivial structure might allow some entities that could satisfy the narrowest
definitions of life: persistent matter that consumes material to sustain or grow itself, have some
limited motility, then replicate copies of itself, before it can no longer sustain the necessary
reactions to do so. Then the material produced by these self-sustaining entities grow the structure
even more, that would then evolve to the topography that supports the present state of the cosmos.

### Chaotic Planes

This doesn't mean there aren't any Chaotic Planes at all anymore. To scholars that discover planar
travel and obervation, they would see these planes and maybe create a model based on our previous
Glacial one.
